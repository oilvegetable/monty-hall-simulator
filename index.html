    Rules & calculation

    Host rules

    Informed
    Knows the car's location and opens a random unchosen goat door.
    Uninformed
    Opens a random unchosen door. Revealing the car stops the game.
    Error-prone
    Uses the uninformed rule with probability e on each opening, and the informed rule otherwise.

    Staying and switching

    One car is placed uniformly among N doors. The initial choice is independent of its location. The host opens R distinct, unchosen doors in sequence.

    After R goat reveals, staying keeps the initial door. Switching selects one other closed door uniformly at random. If more than two doors remain, both strategies can lose.

    Probability calculation

    Let m = N − R − 1 be the alternative doors left. If the initial choice is wrong, the probability of all R host openings revealing goats is:

    s = ∏j = 0…R − 1 (1 − e / (N − 1 − j))

    Use e = 0 for an informed host and e = 1 for an uninformed host. Conditional on all reveals being goats:

    P(stay wins) = 1 / (1 + (N − 1)s)

    P(switch wins) = (1 − P(stay wins)) / m

    With an informed host and R = N − 2, switching wins with probability (N − 1) / N. With an uninformed host, both strategies have probability 1 / (N − R), conditional on safe reveals.

    Simulations check these specified rules. They are not observations from a television show. Gill (2011) discusses the assumptions behind the problem.
