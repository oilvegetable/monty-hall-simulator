    ASSUMPTIONS & CALCULATION

    What the model includes

    The game

    One car is placed uniformly among N doors. The initial choice is independent of its location. The host opens R distinct, unchosen doors in sequence. If the car appears, the game stops.

    After R goat reveals, staying keeps the initial door. Switching selects one other closed door uniformly at random. With more than two closed doors, both strategies can lose.

    The host

    Informed: always chooses uniformly among available goat doors.

    Uninformed: chooses uniformly among all available unchosen doors.

    Error-prone: on each opening, uses the uninformed rule with probability e and the informed rule otherwise. This random action rate is not the same as the probability of revealing the car.

    The probabilities

    Let m = N − R − 1 be the alternatives left after the host finishes. If the initial choice is wrong, the chance that every opening is safe is:

    s = ∏j = 0…R − 1 (1 − e / (N − 1 − j))

    Use e = 0 for an informed host and e = 1 for an uninformed host. Conditional on all R reveals being goats:

    P(stay wins) = 1 / (1 + (N − 1)s)

    P(switch wins) = (1 − P(stay wins)) / m

    For an informed host opening N − 2 doors, switching wins with probability (N − 1) / N. For an uninformed host, both strategies win with probability 1 / (N − R), given safe reveals.

    Simulations use a seeded pseudorandom generator. They check this specified model; they are not observations from a television game show. For discussion of the modelling assumptions, see Gill (2011).
